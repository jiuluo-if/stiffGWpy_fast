## 1. Stojimirovic & Bremer — accelerated frequency-independent solver

- **Bibliography:** Tara Stojimirovic and James Bremer, “An accelerated frequency-independent solver for oscillatory differential equations.” *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis*, published 11 February 2026, DOI [10.1093/imanum/draf128](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/draf128). arXiv [2409.18487v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18487v2), revised 11 March 2025. The paper is also available from the [authors’ PDF](https://www.math.toronto.edu/bremer/papers/riciter.pdf).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/stojimirovic_bremer_2025_frequency_independent.pdf), SHA-256 `5c948d2e816ffedc5df8a4139152bdaa379099d3219835786731e745501bf5ed`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 2.2–2.4, 5.1–5.4, 6.3; equations (1)–(7), (25)–(30), (121)–(124), (127)–(131). The official journal record confirms the publication date and DOI.
- **Method:** For `y'' + ω²q(t,ω)y = 0`, construct a slowly varying phase through a nonoscillatory solution of the logarithmic-derivative Riccati equation. A Chebyshev collocation discretization plus Newton–Kantorovich solves the high-frequency portions; the modulus `m = 1/α'` satisfies Appell’s third-order linear ODE `m''' + 4ω²q m' + 2ω²q_t m = 0`, which extends the representation through low-frequency intervals. A left-to-right and right-to-left sweep adaptively splits intervals when the phase derivative is not spectrally resolved; the phase is then integrated spectrally.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The principal nonoscillatory Riccati existence result assumes smooth bounded coefficient derivatives and a positive lower bound on `q` at sufficiently high frequency. The adaptive algorithm handles low-frequency portions with Appell continuation; the text says negative-`q` intervals require modifications. Appell solutions can grow substantially, and phase evaluation remains limited by the condition number of the represented solution; exponentiating phase values loses accuracy as frequency rises. The reported asymptotic cost is frequency independent for its stated class, not for arbitrary nonsmooth coefficients.
- **Project mapping:** The Fast transfer contains oscillatory and nonoscillatory regimes separated by a horizon-scale turning structure, so a global positive-`q` theorem cannot be applied without deriving the exact transformed coefficient and treating the turning point. The piecewise reheating background also requires exact segment matching. The paper’s Riccati/Chebyshev/Appell family is close to earlier project prototypes (Kummer/Chebyshev phase fitting, Newton–Kantorovich phase solves, and Appell propagation); those prototypes failed on useful-window residual, stability, or cost gates. Reopening the family requires a materially different coefficient scaling or boundary construction and must first pass the existing exact transfer/reference gate.
- **Evidence status:** Full algorithm sections read; equations and assumptions transcribed. No paper code was imported or run in this note.

## 2. Chow & Bremer — Airy phase functions

- **Bibliography:** Richard Chow and James Bremer, “Airy Phase Functions.” arXiv [2503.02306v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02306v2), revised 2 June 2025; arXiv DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2503.02306](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.02306). I did not verify a publisher DOI in this search.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/chow_bremer_2025_airy_phase.pdf), SHA-256 `2bb06a15f4d728f9f74001813240532fa5a18383cc2b1da10a4ced90c903fea2`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 3, 4.1–4.3, and the stated scope/limitations in §6; equations (1), (21), (46)–(48), (108)–(112), and (132)–(134).
- **Method:** For a simple turning point, write the coefficient as `q(t)=t q₀(t)` with smooth positive `q₀`. An Airy phase `γ` makes `Bi(γ)/sqrt(γ')` and `Ai(γ)/sqrt(γ')` a basis. The method obtains a local solution of the Airy–Kummer equation by Chebyshev spectral discretization and Newton iteration near the turn, then extends a piecewise Chebyshev representation over the domain. The asymptotic construction has residual `O(ω⁻²ᴹ)` for its `M`-term expansion; convergence is proven for sufficiently large `ω` under the paper’s conditions.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The principal analysis treats one simple turning point with smooth coefficient and bounded derivatives independent of `ω`; the local interval must be chosen around the turn. The paper notes that numerical generalizations to `q(t) ~ t^σ` for `σ > -2` are broader than its proof, which covers only `σ=-1,0,1`. Higher-order turning points are left for future analysis. Conditioning still limits accuracy as frequency increases.
- **Project mapping:** A simple horizon crossing is a plausible local match after deriving the exact Fast mode equation in its transformed coordinate. The reheating kink is a separate background event and cannot be smoothed into the Airy turn; it needs exact piecewise matching. The project’s earlier composite Airy/WKB transfer experiment is not equivalent to constructing an Airy phase via the Airy–Kummer equation, but it was rejected on accuracy/cost. This paper merits a standalone prototype only after the transformed coefficient and kink interaction are derived.
- **Evidence status:** Algorithmic full-text sections read; no publisher record or code repository was verified.

## 3. Hu & Bremer — frequency-independent solver for first-order systems

- **Bibliography:** Tony Hu and James Bremer, arXiv title “A frequency-independent solver for systems of first order linear ordinary differential equations,” [2309.13848v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13848v1), 25 September 2023. Published as “A frequency-independent solver for systems of linear ordinary differential equations,” *Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics* 470 (2025), 116696, DOI [10.1016/j.cam.2025.116696](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cam.2025.116696).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/hu_bremer_2023_systems_solver.pdf), SHA-256 `c5440d40007d5798699ad3571c9761dedbfeacb244c9fea725859bb42dd4ea3d`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1–2, 4–5; equations (1), (3)–(5), and (14)–(22). The official ScienceDirect record was checked for the published title, venue, volume, article number, and DOI.
- **Method:** Reduce `y'=A(t)y` to a scalar higher-order equation using a cyclic-vector transformation `z=Φy`, where `Φ` is built from a vector and its iterated covariant derivatives. Apply scalar slowly varying phase methods to the resulting equation, then transform the fundamental solution back to the original system.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The paper assumes distinct eigenvalues on each interval (nondegeneracy), smooth matrix entries, and access to derivatives up to the system dimension. Turning points require partitioning into nondegenerate subintervals. The transformation must be well-conditioned; the authors note that selecting a suitable cyclic vector becomes harder with dimension and conditioning drives accuracy loss. Their experiments show deteriorating accuracy at very high frequency and for larger systems.
- **Project mapping:** The tensor propagator is two-dimensional, which is the favorable size for cyclic-vector construction, but its horizon turn is exactly where eigenvalues can coalesce; a scalarization would need a separate turn treatment. Piecewise background derivatives at reheating are another concern. The current Cartesian 2×2 Fast transfer already avoids basis conditioning in its hot loop, so this is not a drop-in speed win. A prototype must prove a well-conditioned transform over a full smooth segment and compare its cost with the current kernel.
- **Evidence status:** Full algorithm and numerical sections read. The paper’s Fortran implementation was not located or run.

## 4. Bremer — phase functions with turning points

- **Bibliography:** James Bremer, “Phase function methods for second order linear ordinary differential equations with turning points.” arXiv [2209.14561v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14561v2), revised 16 December 2022. Published in *Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis* 65 (2023), 137–169, DOI [10.1016/j.acha.2023.02.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2023.02.005).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/bremer_2022_turning_points.pdf), SHA-256 `708171a7e5b6d4563b733f45c4b41dce64719128f256c282e01cfa79a53141c8`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 2, 4.1–4.3, 5.4, 5.7, and 6; equations (23)–(24), (60)–(63), (79)–(82), plus the turning-point experiments. The official Elsevier record was checked for publication metadata.
- **Method:** For smooth `q` with a turn `c`, `q(t) ~ C(t-c)^k`, construct phase functions as piecewise Chebyshev expansions. On the oscillatory side, use a windowing procedure to obtain boundary data; propagate `w=1/α'` through Appell’s third-order linear ODE across the turn/nonoscillatory side. Odd-order turns admit one phase; even-order turns may require separate left and right phases. Multiple turns can be handled by repeated application.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The core algorithm assumes a smooth coefficient and a known turn location/order. The algorithm can truncate when `w` exceeds `10^300`, corresponding to `α'` nearing underflow. Even-order turns do not generally support one globally nonoscillatory phase; separate charts are required. The paper’s experiments cover multiple turns, but its main construction depends on smoothness and boundary data on each segment.
- **Project mapping:** The horizon crossing is a natural simple-turn candidate, while the reheating kink is not itself a smooth turning point and must remain an exact segment boundary. The current transfer already has correct kink splitting. This paper is more directly relevant than a WKB/Airy composite because it computes a numerical phase and uses Appell continuation, but it is close to prior Kummer/Prüfer/Appell prototypes; any new work must identify a distinct boundary/initialization strategy and pass an independent full-mode screen.
- **Evidence status:** Algorithm, even-order discussion, and multi-turn experiments read. No source implementation was located or run.

## 5. Agocs & Barnett — adaptive spectral Riccati defect correction

- **Bibliography:** Fruzsina J. Agocs and Alex H. Barnett, “An Adaptive Spectral Method for Oscillatory Second-Order Linear ODEs with Frequency-Independent Cost,” *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* 62(1) (2024), 295–321, DOI [10.1137/23M1546609](https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1546609). arXiv [2212.06924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06924), submitted 13 December 2022.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/agocs_barnett_2024_adaptive_spectral_ardc.pdf), SHA-256 `077e07601191a250a650635bb21a59c081bf50316aa9c7c3e503f0fa4c8138db`.
- **Read:** Full algorithm sections §2.1–2.6, analysis §3 (especially Theorem 4 and residual proposition), numerical results §4; equations (10)–(24), (27), (30)–(37), and (40)–(41). Cross-checked the SIAM publication record and DOI.
- **Method:** For `u''+2γu'+ω²u=0`, solve the Riccati equation `x'+x²+2γx+ω²=0`; start with `x₀=iω` and apply the pointwise defect correction `x_{j+1}=x_j−R[x_j]/(2(x_j+γ))`. Use a Chebyshev differentiation matrix (the paper uses 16th degree in its experiments), monitor nodal residual reduction, integrate the phase with Clenshaw–Curtis, and adapt step size. Where the asymptotic step fails or is nonoscillatory, switch to standard Chebyshev collocation.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The residual-reduction theorem is local and assumes analytic `ω,γ` in a complex ball, a positive lower bound on `|ω|`, and small derivatives/damping satisfying explicit bounds. The defect series is asymptotic, not generally convergent; discretized roundoff can dominate after a few iterations, and the paper explicitly excludes deeply evanescent dynamic ranges from its analysis. Piecewise-smooth coefficients can be patched at breakpoints, but each smooth interval still needs its own valid local step decisions.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The transformed tensor mode away from a horizon turn can be written in this damped scalar form, with reheating kept as an exact segment boundary and the turn handled by the nonoscillatory spectral branch. This is the same ARDC/Riccati-Chebyshev family already represented in the project’s Phase 6c work, not a new proposal. The current evidence has residual-level candidate modes but no complete accepted integral/root map, and its held-out candidate set remains incomplete. Do not restart with a new implementation label; any continuation must finish full-spectrum/map/root, convergence, and speed gates on the existing prototype.
- **Evidence status:** Equations and assumptions were read from the full arXiv HTML and downloaded PDF. No external code was imported or run for this entry.

## 6. Piron & Tacu — explicit Numerov scheme for Milne phase-amplitude

- **Bibliography:** Robin Piron and Mikael Tacu, “An Explicit Numerical Scheme for Milne’s Phase–Amplitude Equations,” *Atoms* 13(6) (2025), 57, DOI [10.3390/atoms13060057](https://doi.org/10.3390/atoms13060057). arXiv [2411.02621v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02621), submitted 4 November 2024. The publisher PDF gives the 2025 publication history and CC BY license.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/piron_tacu_2025_milne_phase_amplitude.pdf), SHA-256 `2ed61df89e36060c732e729d0b3a7056b7aa79fd97ab7e9b548a6bf60888e02e`.
- **Read:** Full derivation and method §§II–IV, tests §V, conclusion §VI; equations (12), (35)–(39), (44), (48)–(49), (54), (56), and (59). Cross-checked the final MDPI article and DOI.
- **Method:** For the radial Schrödinger equation, Milne gives `φ'=θ/A²` and `A''+ωA−θ²/A³=0`. Setting `Y=A²` yields the linear Appell equation `Y'''+4ωY'+2ω'Y=0` (Eq. 37). The method propagates an auxiliary derivative system with Numerov-like recurrences: sixth order for `Y₁`, fifth order for the main amplitude `Y₀`; a modified predictor-corrector is used on coarse outer intervals. Near the origin the amplitude is singular, so the paper instead propagates the radial wavefunction with Numerov, matches to an outer Bessel/Coulomb boundary solution, and integrates the phase afterward.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The construction is a boundary/matching algorithm for one-dimensional radial Schrödinger problems, not a generic transfer-matrix IVP. It requires boundary data and a matching point; the intermediate recurrence can amplify error when `h²|ω|` or `h²|ω'|` approaches one, so a different outer scheme is required. The paper’s tests show error accumulation is strongest in the intermediate region and report no general global error theorem; the main amplitude recurrence is fifth order.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The linear third-order equation is the same Appell/Milne representation already present in prior project prototypes; the paper contributes a specific Numerov/predictor-corrector discretization and boundary treatment, not a new state variable. Its origin-regular and scattering-boundary construction does not directly transfer to cosmological tensor modes across a horizon turn and reheating kink. A project prototype would first need a precise pair of complex fundamental-solution boundary conditions and a full-segment positivity/conditioning analysis; none is justified by this paper alone.
- **Evidence status:** Full HTML and publisher PDF were inspected and the equations above were extracted. No source implementation was found or run.

## 7. Rosemeier & Klein — WKB-related micro-window time stepping

- **Bibliography:** Juliane Rosemeier and Rupert Klein, “A WKB-related time-stepping scheme for differential equations describing oscillatory systems,” arXiv [2606.13807v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13807), submitted 11 June 2026 (updated 15 June 2026), DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2606.13807](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.13807). This is a preprint; no journal publication was verified.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/rosemeier_klein_2026_wkb_multiscale_timestep.pdf), SHA-256 `969941f5cfc2aa1951b05967f7cb703b4327ebd33925f8ddb9a32286c4edfad5`.
- **Read:** §§2–5 and 6, Algorithms 1–2 and pseudocode appendix D; equations (1)–(6), (12)–(15), (20)–(27), (30)–(32), and the error/cost discussion in §5. The arXiv record gives CC BY 4.0.
- **Method:** The model class is `ẏ=ε⁻¹ L y+N(y)` with a fixed real skew-symmetric `L` and weak nonlinear remainder. Express the solution in its fixed eigenbasis with modal amplitudes and nonlinear phases. The scheme runs a short RK4 micro-simulation spanning a few periods, extracts amplitudes/phases from Cartesian samples, smooths them where higher-order oscillations remain, linearly fits their slow trends, extrapolates across a larger macro step, and reconstructs the Cartesian state. Its cost is bounded as `ε→0` for a fixed coarse-step count when both micro-window length and micro-step scale with `ε`.
- **Accuracy and failure limits:** The extrapolation is first order in the coarse step. The zero-eigenvalue example removes fast oscillations in transformed coordinates, but the van der Pol transformed system retains fast terms at `O(ε)`; the authors report that error generally cannot be reduced below `O(ε)` without a higher-order transformation. A vanishing modal amplitude makes its phase undefined. Tests cover two weakly nonlinear systems with fixed linear operators; they do not establish a global error bound for the project’s time-varying linear coefficient.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The tensor mode is a linear time-varying oscillator with a real horizon turn and a piecewise reheating event, not a fixed `L/ε` plus weak nonlinear autonomous remainder. A local interaction-picture split might create such a form on a smooth oscillatory segment, but its residual must remain uniformly small and the basis must stay conditioned near the turn; this has not been shown. Micro-window extrapolation is distinct from direct WKB phase transport, but it requires repeated short solves and slow-trend fits, which may not beat the batched Numba transfer or retain the full sampled spectrum. Do not start a prototype until a coefficient split is derived that covers the connected oscillatory interval and exact event matching.
- **Evidence status:** Full arXiv HTML and downloaded PDF were read. No author code repository was found or run; this is an equation-level applicability assessment, not a numerical reproduction.

## 8. Arnold, Ben Abdallah & Negulescu — asymptotic-preserving WKB finite-difference schemes

- **Bibliography:** Anton Arnold, Naoufel Ben Abdallah, and Claudia Negulescu, “WKB-Based Schemes for the Oscillatory 1D Schrödinger Equation in the Semiclassical Limit,” *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* 49(4) (2011), 1436–1460, DOI [10.1137/100800373](https://doi.org/10.1137/100800373). I read the authors’ 4 May 2010 manuscript hosted on the coauthor’s university page; the SIAM record confirms the final publication metadata. No arXiv version was identified.
- **Local source:** [Author manuscript PDF](sources/arnold_ben_abdallah_negulescu_2011_wkb_schrodinger.pdf), SHA-256 `162ece6741a50a339d2b98662e8992598fee4c237b8f8e2884fd0b23ad537862`.
- **Read:** Full manuscript §§1–3, especially the WKB transform §§2.1–2.2 and error analysis Theorem 3.1; equations (1.1)–(1.5), (2.2)–(2.10), (2.15)–(2.34), and (3.1)–(3.4). SIAM publisher abstract and final bibliographic record were cross-checked.
- **Method:** For `ε²ϕ''+a(x)ϕ=0`, use a second-order WKB phase with `β=a''/(8a^{3/2})−5(a')²/(32a^{5/2})`, transform through a first-order system and diagonalize the leading oscillatory operator, then remove its phase. The transformed state satisfies `Z'=εN_εZ` and stays `O(ε²)` from its initial adiabatic invariant. A Picard expansion with integration-by-parts / trigonometric corrections yields first- and second-order finite-difference updates for the transformed state. The second-order transformed-state error is `O(ε³h²)`.
- **Assumptions and limits:** Hypothesis A requires a fixed smooth real `a(x)≥a₀>0` on the entire interval: a uniformly oscillatory regime with no turn or evanescent section. Piecewise-smooth coefficients may be restarted at jumps. The original wavefunction error also includes phase quadrature error `O(h^γ/ε)`, which can dominate at small ε unless the phase is integrated exactly or at sufficiently high order. In generic cases the paper’s phase quadrature constraint gives `h=O(√ε)`; fixed coarse `h` independent of ε is justified only in special cases with explicitly integrable phase.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** After removing tensor damping, the deep subhorizon equation has a leading `k²C(N)` term, so setting `ε=1/k` suggests a possible Schrödinger-like segment. This is an inference, not a proven equivalence: the scaled coefficient’s positive lower bound is not uniform near the horizon entry, and reheating remains a real breakpoint. The approach overlaps the existing WKB/phase/transfer-map experiments but offers a distinct asymptotic-preserving finite-difference correction in a strictly oscillatory segment. It cannot replace the exact turn treatment or current tail handoff by itself; implementation is not justified until the uniform coefficient bounds and full-mode performance are established.
- **Evidence status:** Full author manuscript and equation sections were read. No reference implementation was found or run; no numerical reproduction is claimed.

## 9. Perczyński & Madejski — Neumann-series integrator with Filon quadrature

- **Bibliography:** Rafał Perczyński and Grzegorz Madejski, “Numerical integrator for highly oscillatory differential equations based on the Neumann series,” *Numerical Algorithms* 98 (2025), 1481–1505, published online 25 April 2024, DOI [10.1007/s11075-024-01841-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-024-01841-9). arXiv [2311.07142v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07142), 19 June 2024. The Springer record marks the final paper open access.
- **Local source:** [arXiv PDF](sources/perczynski_madejski_2025_neumann_filon.pdf), SHA-256 `64e3cb3ddb68648140ac53b847a6305efe3ecaea43ae89f289928586934b1e34`.
- **Read:** Full sections 1–7, with emphasis on §3 derivation, §4 positive/negative-frequency error analysis, and §5–6 examples; equations (1)–(18), (16)–(19), and Theorem 1. Cross-checked final title, authors, journal, pages, date, DOI, and open-access status on Springer.
- **Method:** For a linear evolution equation `u_t=Lu+f(t)u` with `f(x,t)=Σ α_n(x,t)e^{inωt}`, use Duhamel’s formula to expand the solution as a Neumann/Dyson series around the semigroup `e^{hL}`. Truncate after the first few terms and approximate the resulting one-, two-, and three-dimensional simplex integrals by Hermite/Filon rules with explicitly computed oscillatory moments. The paper’s scheme is third order globally (fourth-order local error) and the positive-frequency local bound improves like `min(h⁴,h²ω⁻²,ω⁻³)` under its regularity and nonresonance assumptions. Negative-frequency resonances require a paired-integral correction.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The potential must be a finite Fourier sum in an external time frequency with sufficiently smooth amplitudes; the spatial operator is a bounded-domain elliptic generator with specified boundary conditions and semigroup. Higher Neumann truncation needs more coefficient/initial-data regularity and adds nested integral cost. The method is tailored to oscillatory *multiplicative forcing*, not to a scalar homogeneous mode whose fast oscillation comes from its principal coefficient.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The project has no spatial elliptic operator and no imposed finite Fourier series in time. Its tensor equation has a time-varying homogeneous 2×2 generator, a horizon crossing, and an exact reheating event. A frozen-generator interaction-picture expansion could in principle create an oscillatory perturbation, but that would need a new controlled split and would overlap the already tested Magnus/Duhamel/linear-z transfer-map family; Round 64 Duhamel correction failed its accuracy/runtime gates. No direct transfer of the published PDE method is justified.
- **Evidence status:** Full arXiv HTML and PDF were read. The paper provides no project-compatible implementation to run; no numerical reproduction is claimed.

## 10. Blanes, Casas, Oteo & Ros — Magnus expansion and applications

- **Bibliography:** Sergio Blanes, Fernando Casas, José A. Oteo, and José Ros, “The Magnus expansion and some of its applications,” *Physics Reports* 470(5–6) (2009), 151–238, DOI [10.1016/j.physrep.2008.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2008.11.001). arXiv [0810.5488v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.5488), submitted 30 October 2008.
- **Local source:** [arXiv PDF](sources/blanes_casas_oteo_ros_2009_magnus_review.pdf), SHA-256 `01f4fa9e8356fd14fd843ccbb0c56e23bea219e0ff99dc5ffea9216dee363e10`. The PDF header and extracted text were checked; the arXiv HTML and publisher record confirm the version and final journal metadata.
- **Read:** PDF §§2.1–2.7 (Magnus theorem, first terms, time symmetry, convergence), §5.4 (linear Magnus integrators and quadrature), §5.6 (matrix exponentials), §5.8.2 (commutator-free methods), and §6.1.1.3 (oscillatory Schrödinger example); especially equations (40), (67)–(69), (240)–(247), (289)–(295), and the order/cost discussion in §§5.4 and 5.6. Cross-checked against the full arXiv HTML and the *Physics Reports* publisher page.
- **Method and guarantees:** For `Y'=A(t)Y`, write `Y(t)=exp(Ω(t))Y₀`; `Ω₁=∫A`, `Ω₂=½∫∫[A(t₁),A(t₂)]`, followed by higher nested-commutator integrals. The Lie-algebra form preserves corresponding group structure (for example, symplecticity) at each truncation order. A sufficient convergence condition is `∫||A(s)||ds<π`; it is not necessary, and the review emphasizes that the Magnus *series* is generally only locally convergent. Numerical methods split the interval, truncate the series, approximate its integrals, then evaluate one or more matrix exponentials. Fourth-order commutator-free schemes avoid explicit nested commutators but use multiple exponentials per step.
- **Cost and project fit:** The review explicitly notes that matrix-exponential work can dominate total cost; commutator-free composition reduces commutator algebra but does not remove exponential work or the need for short steps when the generator norm is large. A custom traceless 2×2 exponential can be evaluated in closed form, as used by project prototypes, but this only addresses exponential overhead. The tensor generator has a turning region and a piecewise reheating event, so a generic high-frequency Magnus truncation still needs a valid step/convergence or error-control argument on each smooth segment and exact event splitting.
- **Deduplication and result:** This is foundational context, not a new algorithm for this project. The repository already has a linear-z second-order Magnus transfer-map experiment; its closed 2×2/Numba version took 4.72–5.01 ms versus 2.56–2.65 ms for baseline across five regimes (about 1.8–2.0× the time) and was rejected. The Gaussian-node CF4 prototype's measured candidate medians were 1.42–1.63× the baseline at its unfiltered setting; its independent-oracle errors remained essentially unchanged. The paired-commutator Magnus experiment also exists in `pair_magnus_round63_20260923.json`; previous Duhamel correction is recorded as failing accuracy/runtime gates. These records preclude presenting another unmodified Magnus/CF4/Duhamel variant as a broad Fast improvement. Sources: `transfer_map_phase_assessment.md`, `transfer_map_phase_numba_round.json`, `cf4_magnus_round32_kernel_20260923.json`, `cf4_magnus_round32_oracle_20260923.json`, and `pair_magnus_round63_20260923.json`.
- **Evidence status:** The review's stated convergence assumptions, constructions, and numerical-method sections were read from the full arXiv PDF/HTML. The project applicability assessment is an inference from the project's existing transfer kernels and archived A/B results. No new implementation or numerical reproduction is claimed.

If the Kolmogorov widths satisfy `d_n(F)≤M n^{-α}`, Theorem 3.1 gives greedy errors `σ_n(F)≤C(α,γ)M n^{-α}`. Exponential width decay also implies a greedy decay rate, with a weaker exponent `α/(α+1)` for the stated weak-greedy bound. With snapshot perturbations bounded by `ε`, Theorem 3.4 gives an error floor of order `max(M n^{-α}, ε)`. For arbitrary compact sets, Theorem 4.4's exact-greedy comparison contains a factor of order `2^n`; the constructed example shows such exponential separation can be nearly attained. Thus the results are conditional on solution-manifold width decay and selection quality, not a universal low-rank guarantee.

A practical residual surrogate qualifies only when two-sided constants comparable to (1.5) are available. Any application must bound full-frequency spectrum and self-consistent-root error, including error amplification, and verify the estimator's `γ` condition across the intended continuous region. A sampled basis alone cannot establish those facts or meet the task's no-unverified-proxy rule. No implementation or production change follows from this theory paper.

## 13. Jakeman & Wildey — adjoint error estimates for adaptive sparse grids

- **Bibliography:** John D. Jakeman and Timothy Wildey, “Enhancing adaptive sparse grid approximations and improving refinement strategies using adjoint-based a posteriori error estimates,” *Journal of Computational Physics* 280 (2015), 54–71, DOI [10.1016/j.jcp.2014.09.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2014.09.014). arXiv [1407.1061v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1061), 3 July 2014; Sandia report SAND2013-10657J.

- **Method:** For a chosen quantity of interest `J(ξ)` of a parameterized PDE/ODE solution, solve a backward adjoint with terminal condition set by the output functional. The error identity (3.3) writes functional error as an initial-data term plus the forward weighted residual against the adjoint, with a nonlinear remainder. For a sparse-grid surrogate, approximate the forward state, adjoint, and weighted residual over parameter space; equation (5.1) estimates each surrogate sample's QoI error. Weighted absolute estimates then drive dimension/local refinement (6.1)–(6.2), can correct the surrogate, and can balance physical-discretization error against parameter-interpolation error. The paper demonstrates the framework on a parameterized linear system, 25-dimensional diffusion, and a 9-dimensional nonlinear predator–prey ODE.
- **Assumptions, costs, and limits:** The sample estimator is approximate; for smooth problems its estimator error is higher order in the forward/adjoint approximation errors, and the nonlinear remainder is neglected or treated as higher order. Evaluating it requires the forward and adjoint approximations plus a weighted residual, can require storing sparse-grid surpluses for every space/time state component, and the authors state that this cost is nontrivial. They therefore approximate the error itself from a finite set of estimates. The reported advantage is fewer redundant parameter-model evaluations on high-dimensional, anisotropic examples; the paper does not give a universal continuous-domain certificate for arbitrary QoIs.

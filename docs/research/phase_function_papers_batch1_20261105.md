## 1. Stojimirovic & Bremer — accelerated frequency-independent solver

- **Bibliography:** Tara Stojimirovic and James Bremer, “An accelerated frequency-independent solver for oscillatory differential equations.” *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis*, published 11 February 2026, DOI [10.1093/imanum/draf128](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/draf128). arXiv [2409.18487v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18487v2), revised 11 March 2025. The paper is also available from the [authors’ PDF](https://www.math.toronto.edu/bremer/papers/riciter.pdf).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/stojimirovic_bremer_2025_frequency_independent.pdf), SHA-256 `5c948d2e816ffedc5df8a4139152bdaa379099d3219835786731e745501bf5ed`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 2.2–2.4, 5.1–5.4, 6.3; equations (1)–(7), (25)–(30), (121)–(124), (127)–(131). The official journal record confirms the publication date and DOI.
- **Method:** For `y'' + ω²q(t,ω)y = 0`, construct a slowly varying phase through a nonoscillatory solution of the logarithmic-derivative Riccati equation. A Chebyshev collocation discretization plus Newton–Kantorovich solves the high-frequency portions; the modulus `m = 1/α'` satisfies Appell’s third-order linear ODE `m''' + 4ω²q m' + 2ω²q_t m = 0`, which extends the representation through low-frequency intervals. A left-to-right and right-to-left sweep adaptively splits intervals when the phase derivative is not spectrally resolved; the phase is then integrated spectrally.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The principal nonoscillatory Riccati existence result assumes smooth bounded coefficient derivatives and a positive lower bound on `q` at sufficiently high frequency. The adaptive algorithm handles low-frequency portions with Appell continuation; the text says negative-`q` intervals require modifications. Appell solutions can grow substantially, and phase evaluation remains limited by the condition number of the represented solution; exponentiating phase values loses accuracy as frequency rises. The reported asymptotic cost is frequency independent for its stated class, not for arbitrary nonsmooth coefficients.
- **Project mapping:** The Fast transfer contains oscillatory and nonoscillatory regimes separated by a horizon-scale turning structure, so a global positive-`q` theorem cannot be applied without deriving the exact transformed coefficient and treating the turning point. The piecewise reheating background also requires exact segment matching. The paper’s Riccati/Chebyshev/Appell family is close to earlier project prototypes (Kummer/Chebyshev phase fitting, Newton–Kantorovich phase solves, and Appell propagation); those prototypes failed on useful-window residual, stability, or cost gates. Reopening the family requires a materially different coefficient scaling or boundary construction and must first pass the existing exact transfer/reference gate.
- **Evidence status:** Full algorithm sections read; equations and assumptions transcribed. No paper code was imported or run in this note.

## 2. Chow & Bremer — Airy phase functions

- **Bibliography:** Richard Chow and James Bremer, “Airy Phase Functions.” arXiv [2503.02306v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02306v2), revised 2 June 2025; arXiv DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2503.02306](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.02306). I did not verify a publisher DOI in this search.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/chow_bremer_2025_airy_phase.pdf), SHA-256 `2bb06a15f4d728f9f74001813240532fa5a18383cc2b1da10a4ced90c903fea2`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 3, 4.1–4.3, and the stated scope/limitations in §6; equations (1), (21), (46)–(48), (108)–(112), and (132)–(134).
- **Method:** For a simple turning point, write the coefficient as `q(t)=t q₀(t)` with smooth positive `q₀`. An Airy phase `γ` makes `Bi(γ)/sqrt(γ')` and `Ai(γ)/sqrt(γ')` a basis. The method obtains a local solution of the Airy–Kummer equation by Chebyshev spectral discretization and Newton iteration near the turn, then extends a piecewise Chebyshev representation over the domain. The asymptotic construction has residual `O(ω⁻²ᴹ)` for its `M`-term expansion; convergence is proven for sufficiently large `ω` under the paper’s conditions.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The principal analysis treats one simple turning point with smooth coefficient and bounded derivatives independent of `ω`; the local interval must be chosen around the turn. The paper notes that numerical generalizations to `q(t) ~ t^σ` for `σ > -2` are broader than its proof, which covers only `σ=-1,0,1`. Higher-order turning points are left for future analysis. Conditioning still limits accuracy as frequency increases.
- **Project mapping:** A simple horizon crossing is a plausible local match after deriving the exact Fast mode equation in its transformed coordinate. The reheating kink is a separate background event and cannot be smoothed into the Airy turn; it needs exact piecewise matching. The project’s earlier composite Airy/WKB transfer experiment is not equivalent to constructing an Airy phase via the Airy–Kummer equation, but it was rejected on accuracy/cost. This paper merits a standalone prototype only after the transformed coefficient and kink interaction are derived.
- **Evidence status:** Algorithmic full-text sections read; no publisher record or code repository was verified.

## 3. Hu & Bremer — frequency-independent solver for first-order systems

- **Bibliography:** Tony Hu and James Bremer, arXiv title “A frequency-independent solver for systems of first order linear ordinary differential equations,” [2309.13848v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13848v1), 25 September 2023. Published as “A frequency-independent solver for systems of linear ordinary differential equations,” *Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics* 470 (2025), 116696, DOI [10.1016/j.cam.2025.116696](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cam.2025.116696).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/hu_bremer_2023_systems_solver.pdf), SHA-256 `c5440d40007d5798699ad3571c9761dedbfeacb244c9fea725859bb42dd4ea3d`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1–2, 4–5; equations (1), (3)–(5), and (14)–(22). The official ScienceDirect record was checked for the published title, venue, volume, article number, and DOI.
- **Method:** Reduce `y'=A(t)y` to a scalar higher-order equation using a cyclic-vector transformation `z=Φy`, where `Φ` is built from a vector and its iterated covariant derivatives. Apply scalar slowly varying phase methods to the resulting equation, then transform the fundamental solution back to the original system.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The paper assumes distinct eigenvalues on each interval (nondegeneracy), smooth matrix entries, and access to derivatives up to the system dimension. Turning points require partitioning into nondegenerate subintervals. The transformation must be well-conditioned; the authors note that selecting a suitable cyclic vector becomes harder with dimension and conditioning drives accuracy loss. Their experiments show deteriorating accuracy at very high frequency and for larger systems.
- **Project mapping:** The tensor propagator is two-dimensional, which is the favorable size for cyclic-vector construction, but its horizon turn is exactly where eigenvalues can coalesce; a scalarization would need a separate turn treatment. Piecewise background derivatives at reheating are another concern. The current Cartesian 2×2 Fast transfer already avoids basis conditioning in its hot loop, so this is not a drop-in speed win. A prototype must prove a well-conditioned transform over a full smooth segment and compare its cost with the current kernel.
- **Evidence status:** Full algorithm and numerical sections read. The paper’s Fortran implementation was not located or run.

## 4. Bremer — phase functions with turning points

- **Bibliography:** James Bremer, “Phase function methods for second order linear ordinary differential equations with turning points.” arXiv [2209.14561v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14561v2), revised 16 December 2022. Published in *Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis* 65 (2023), 137–169, DOI [10.1016/j.acha.2023.02.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2023.02.005).
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/bremer_2022_turning_points.pdf), SHA-256 `708171a7e5b6d4563b733f45c4b41dce64719128f256c282e01cfa79a53141c8`.
- **Read:** arXiv HTML §§1, 2, 4.1–4.3, 5.4, 5.7, and 6; equations (23)–(24), (60)–(63), (79)–(82), plus the turning-point experiments. The official Elsevier record was checked for publication metadata.
- **Method:** For smooth `q` with a turn `c`, `q(t) ~ C(t-c)^k`, construct phase functions as piecewise Chebyshev expansions. On the oscillatory side, use a windowing procedure to obtain boundary data; propagate `w=1/α'` through Appell’s third-order linear ODE across the turn/nonoscillatory side. Odd-order turns admit one phase; even-order turns may require separate left and right phases. Multiple turns can be handled by repeated application.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The core algorithm assumes a smooth coefficient and a known turn location/order. The algorithm can truncate when `w` exceeds `10^300`, corresponding to `α'` nearing underflow. Even-order turns do not generally support one globally nonoscillatory phase; separate charts are required. The paper’s experiments cover multiple turns, but its main construction depends on smoothness and boundary data on each segment.
- **Project mapping:** The horizon crossing is a natural simple-turn candidate, while the reheating kink is not itself a smooth turning point and must remain an exact segment boundary. The current transfer already has correct kink splitting. This paper is more directly relevant than a WKB/Airy composite because it computes a numerical phase and uses Appell continuation, but it is close to prior Kummer/Prüfer/Appell prototypes; any new work must identify a distinct boundary/initialization strategy and pass an independent full-mode screen.
- **Evidence status:** Algorithm, even-order discussion, and multi-turn experiments read. No source implementation was located or run.

## Cross-paper decision

These papers support a focused next investigation of the exact transformed tensor coefficient and its horizon turn, but none yet establishes that phase construction is cheaper or more accurate than the existing Fast transfer over the project’s background family. Paper 1 and paper 4 largely overlap prior Kummer/Appell/Prüfer experiments. Paper 2 is the most distinct turn-aware representation; paper 3 offers a system-to-scalar route but is exposed to eigenvalue coalescence and transform conditioning. No production change follows from literature alone.

## 5. Agocs & Barnett — adaptive spectral Riccati defect correction

- **Bibliography:** Fruzsina J. Agocs and Alex H. Barnett, “An Adaptive Spectral Method for Oscillatory Second-Order Linear ODEs with Frequency-Independent Cost,” *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* 62(1) (2024), 295–321, DOI [10.1137/23M1546609](https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1546609). arXiv [2212.06924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06924), submitted 13 December 2022.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/agocs_barnett_2024_adaptive_spectral_ardc.pdf), SHA-256 `077e07601191a250a650635bb21a59c081bf50316aa9c7c3e503f0fa4c8138db`.
- **Read:** Full algorithm sections §2.1–2.6, analysis §3 (especially Theorem 4 and residual proposition), numerical results §4; equations (10)–(24), (27), (30)–(37), and (40)–(41). Cross-checked the SIAM publication record and DOI.
- **Method:** For `u''+2γu'+ω²u=0`, solve the Riccati equation `x'+x²+2γx+ω²=0`; start with `x₀=iω` and apply the pointwise defect correction `x_{j+1}=x_j−R[x_j]/(2(x_j+γ))`. Use a Chebyshev differentiation matrix (the paper uses 16th degree in its experiments), monitor nodal residual reduction, integrate the phase with Clenshaw–Curtis, and adapt step size. Where the asymptotic step fails or is nonoscillatory, switch to standard Chebyshev collocation.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The residual-reduction theorem is local and assumes analytic `ω,γ` in a complex ball, a positive lower bound on `|ω|`, and small derivatives/damping satisfying explicit bounds. The defect series is asymptotic, not generally convergent; discretized roundoff can dominate after a few iterations, and the paper explicitly excludes deeply evanescent dynamic ranges from its analysis. Piecewise-smooth coefficients can be patched at breakpoints, but each smooth interval still needs its own valid local step decisions.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The transformed tensor mode away from a horizon turn can be written in this damped scalar form, with reheating kept as an exact segment boundary and the turn handled by the nonoscillatory spectral branch. This is the same ARDC/Riccati-Chebyshev family already represented in the project’s Phase 6c work, not a new proposal. The current evidence has residual-level candidate modes but no complete accepted integral/root map, and its held-out candidate set remains incomplete. Do not restart with a new implementation label; any continuation must finish full-spectrum/map/root, convergence, and speed gates on the existing prototype.
- **Evidence status:** Equations and assumptions were read from the full arXiv HTML and downloaded PDF. No external code was imported or run for this entry.

## 6. Piron & Tacu — explicit Numerov scheme for Milne phase-amplitude

- **Bibliography:** Robin Piron and Mikael Tacu, “An Explicit Numerical Scheme for Milne’s Phase–Amplitude Equations,” *Atoms* 13(6) (2025), 57, DOI [10.3390/atoms13060057](https://doi.org/10.3390/atoms13060057). arXiv [2411.02621v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02621), submitted 4 November 2024. The publisher PDF gives the 2025 publication history and CC BY license.
- **Local source:** [PDF](sources/piron_tacu_2025_milne_phase_amplitude.pdf), SHA-256 `2ed61df89e36060c732e729d0b3a7056b7aa79fd97ab7e9b548a6bf60888e02e`.
- **Read:** Full derivation and method §§II–IV, tests §V, conclusion §VI; equations (12), (35)–(39), (44), (48)–(49), (54), (56), and (59). Cross-checked the final MDPI article and DOI.
- **Method:** For the radial Schrödinger equation, Milne gives `φ'=θ/A²` and `A''+ωA−θ²/A³=0`. Setting `Y=A²` yields the linear Appell equation `Y'''+4ωY'+2ω'Y=0` (Eq. 37). The method propagates an auxiliary derivative system with Numerov-like recurrences: sixth order for `Y₁`, fifth order for the main amplitude `Y₀`; a modified predictor-corrector is used on coarse outer intervals. Near the origin the amplitude is singular, so the paper instead propagates the radial wavefunction with Numerov, matches to an outer Bessel/Coulomb boundary solution, and integrates the phase afterward.
- **Assumptions and limits:** The construction is a boundary/matching algorithm for one-dimensional radial Schrödinger problems, not a generic transfer-matrix IVP. It requires boundary data and a matching point; the intermediate recurrence can amplify error when `h²|ω|` or `h²|ω'|` approaches one, so a different outer scheme is required. The paper’s tests show error accumulation is strongest in the intermediate region and report no general global error theorem; the main amplitude recurrence is fifth order.
- **Project mapping and deduplication:** The linear third-order equation is the same Appell/Milne representation already present in prior project prototypes; the paper contributes a specific Numerov/predictor-corrector discretization and boundary treatment, not a new state variable. Its origin-regular and scattering-boundary construction does not directly transfer to cosmological tensor modes across a horizon turn and reheating kink. A project prototype would first need a precise pair of complex fundamental-solution boundary conditions and a full-segment positivity/conditioning analysis; none is justified by this paper alone.
- **Evidence status:** Full HTML and publisher PDF were inspected and the equations above were extracted. No source implementation was found or run.
